The pitch is always the same. One platform. Everything in one place. Migrate off
the mess you have and onto the thing that does it all.

Sometimes that is right. Usually it is not.

## The actual problem is translation

Walk into most small operations and you will find good software everywhere.
Accounting is solid. The telematics platform works. The field app the crew uses
does what it says. Nothing is broken.

What is broken is the space between them. A job gets created in one system,
re-typed into a second, and reconciled in a third — by a person, from memory,
at the end of the week. That person is the integration layer. They are also a
single point of failure, and they would like to take a vacation.

## Replacement resets the clock on everything that works

A migration does not just move data. It moves every workaround your team
invented, every report someone depends on, and every habit that makes the
current process survivable. You are trading a known problem for an unknown one,
and paying for the privilege in months of reduced output.

Integration is narrower. You are not asking the business to change. You are
asking two systems to agree.

## What "integrated" actually has to mean

The word gets used loosely, so here is the bar we hold:

- A record is entered **once**, at the point it is first known
- Every sync is **monitored** — a failure raises an alert, not a silence
- Failures are **retryable** without creating duplicates
- There is a **runbook** so a broken sync is a known problem with known steps

A nightly script that quietly stops working in March and gets noticed in June is
not an integration. It is a liability with a cron entry.

## When replacement really is the answer

We do recommend it, in three cases:

1. The vendor is gone, or the platform is genuinely end-of-life
2. The system cannot express something the business now fundamentally does
3. The licensing cost has outgrown what a purpose-built tool would cost to own

Outside of that, we would rather make what you have work — and spend the budget
on the part of your operation that no vendor sells.
